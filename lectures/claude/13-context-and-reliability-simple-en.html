Lecture 13: context and reliability (domain 5)

Based on the exam course, domain 5: Context Management & Reliability. Cards: group "Надійність і контекст", test "Домен 5". Caching, compaction and context editing are in lectures 6 and 7.

The main idea

Extract what matters, persist it, trim the rest. The principle works both for a long conversation and for a large codebase. The second half of the domain is about honesty: when to stop and call a human, how to report failures, and where every fact came from.

Step 1. Why long conversations get worse

  • The context window is finite. As it fills, accuracy drops: this is context rot.
  • The model weighs the start and the end most. A fact in the middle can be missed: this is lost in the middle.

What to do:

Technique Point
Trim old tool output Keep the conclusion, drop the 2,000-line raw dump
Summarize older turns This is what /compact does. Say what must survive: a bad summary silently drops a detail
Case-facts block A short block: the customer, the issue, the decisions. Re-inject it at the end of the prompt every turn, where attention is highest

Step 2. A large codebase

Reading a big codebase fills the window just as fast. The tools:

  • Explore subagent: heavy reading in a separate context, only findings come back (lecture 11).
  • Scratchpad files: write findings to disk ("auth lives in auth/, the bug is in login()") and re-read only the note you need.
  • /compact: run it proactively at about 70-75% full, at a natural break, not only when forced.
  • Crash-recovery manifest: a file with the task, what is done and what is next. A refactor crashed at file 40: a new session reads the manifest and carries on instead of starting at file 1.

Step 3. When to call a human

A confident wrong action is worse than asking. An agent that knows its limits is working correctly.

Escalation triggers:

Trigger Example
Low confidence The model is not sure enough to act
High stakes Money, safety, legal matters
Policy gap No rule covers the case. Do not invent a policy
Explicit request "I want to speak to a person": hand over at once, do not talk them out of it

Multiple matches are different. Two customers named Sharma or two orders that fit: ask the user a clarifying question ("Did you mean A or B?"). That is clarification, not a human handoff.

Step 4. Failures in a multi-agent system

The worst failure is a silent one: a subagent returned nothing, the coordinator did not notice and confidently produced an incomplete report.

A subagent hands the coordinator a structured error (the same format as lecture 10: isError, category):

  • what failed and why;
  • the failure type;
  • partial results ("got 2 of 5 sources").

Tell an unreachable source (a real failure, retryable) apart from an empty result (a valid answer, do not retry).

What the coordinator does:

Situation Action
A transient failure (a timeout) Retry
A non-critical part Skip it with a note
A critical part Escalate to a human

The report says honestly what is missing: "Findings complete except news sources, which were unavailable."

Step 5. Human review and confidence

Reviewing everything is impossible, reviewing nothing is risky. Review what is most likely to be wrong.

  • Confidence per field, not per document: a typed policy number is certain, a handwritten total is not. Only the shaky field goes to a human.
  • Routing: high confidence is accepted automatically, low goes to a human.
  • Stratified sampling: spot-check every confidence band, not only the low one. Otherwise you never catch the cases where the model was confidently wrong.
  • Accuracy by document type and field: handwritten forms error-prone, the total field weak? Route those more aggressively and relax on fields that are consistently right.

Step 6. Sources

Blend ten sources into smooth prose and you lose where each fact came from; a fabrication becomes indistinguishable from a finding.

  • Claim-source mapping: every fact keeps its source, like a citation.
  • Surface conflicts: show both versions with their sources, do not silently pick one or average them. Flag what is uncertain.
  • Date every fact (temporal framing): a 2019 figure and a 2026 figure are not equally current. An old fact stated as current is quietly wrong.

What to remember for the exam

  • Lost in the middle: key facts go in a case-facts block at the end of the prompt.
  • Extract, persist, trim. For code: Explore subagent, scratchpad, /compact at 70-75%, a manifest.
  • Escalate on low confidence, high stakes, a policy gap or an explicit request for a human.
  • Multiple matches: a clarifying question to the user.
  • A subagent never hides a failure. Empty does not mean error.
  • Confidence per field, stratified sampling, a source and a date for every fact.

Check yourself

  1. Where do key facts live in a long conversation?
  2. When should you run /compact?
  3. What do you do when no rule covers the case?
  4. What does a subagent that got 2 of 5 sources return?
  5. Why is reviewing only low-confidence results not enough?
Answers
  1. In a case-facts block at the end of the prompt.
  2. Proactively, at about 70-75% full.
  3. Escalate to a human, do not invent a rule.
  4. A structured error with the failure type and partial results.
  5. You miss the confident mistakes.
